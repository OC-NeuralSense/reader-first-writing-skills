# Genres: One Method, Four Settings

The method described in these chapters is universal in its spine. Every genre starts from the same reader, the same governing question, the same insistence on a sound structure rendered in clear prose, and the same non-negotiable fidelity to meaning. What changes between genres is not the method but a handful of its settings: where the answer sits, how much visible scaffolding the reader needs, whether the ending is allowed to push, and which quality lens the reviewer applies most heavily.

This chapter shows how the universal method parameterizes across the four genres this version commits to, and, just as importantly, which of those choices are fixed by the genre and which are settings a human decides case by case.

## What stays constant

Before the differences, the invariants. In every genre:

- A single reader is profiled first, and what they already know sets the depth, the jargon, and the starting ground.
- The document turns on one reducible question, and its parts hang from a real hierarchy in which each parent honestly summarizes its children.
- Sentences are engineered for first-pass comprehension: parseable, coherent, unambiguous, concrete where concreteness helps.
- Every transformation preserves meaning: claims, numbers, dates, negations, modality, conditions, exceptions, qualifications, technical sense, and the writer's position.

No genre setting may override these. A genre tunes the method; it does not suspend the fidelity floor or the reader-comprehension criterion.

## Business and analytical writing

**Shape: answer-first, close on action.** The reader is usually deciding something and has little patience for an approach march. Lead with the answer, then support it. *Example:* a memo on vendor consolidation opens "We should move all three regional contracts to a single supplier by March, cutting annual spend by about 18%," and only then lays out the reasoning. The reader who agrees can stop; the reader who wants the case reads on.

The one principled exception to answer-first lives here: when the reader is expected to resist the conclusion, lead with the reasoning so the case arrives before the unwelcome verdict, rather than provoking a reflexive no. This is a controlled inversion triggered by the reader's expected reaction, not a free choice.

The recommendation must be a real claim, not a topic label: "consolidate to one supplier," not "the vendor question." Support must genuinely establish it, alternatives are argued on their merits rather than by demolishing straw versions, and the document may legitimately close with calibrated force toward the decision it seeks. A policy memo is handled as a sub-case of this genre: same answer-first discipline, same merits-based argument.

## Academic writing

**Shape: soundness above all, with honest handling of the field.** The reader is a competent peer who will test the reasoning, so the highest bar is inferential soundness: every step following, evidence fitting the claim, the treatment complete for the question posed.

Three things distinguish this genre's quality lens. Citations do real work: claims that rest on prior findings are anchored to them, per the conventions of the venue. Counter-positions are handled fairly: set up in their strongest form and then engaged, not caricatured and knocked down. And overclaiming is a specific sin: the conclusion may not outrun the evidence, hedges and scope limits are load-bearing content, and a finding true "in our sample" may not be dressed as a universal law. The presentational stance dominates: the aim is to establish a truth for a discerning equal, so the writing does not emote or sell.

## General explanatory nonfiction

**Shape: comprehension first, no argument to win.** The reader wants to understand something, not to be persuaded of a position or told what to do. The document's success is measured by whether that reader comes away understanding.

So the lens is squarely on grasp. Concepts are pitched to the reader's assumed prior knowledge; the progression runs given-before-new so each idea attaches to one already in hand; concrete and worked material appears wherever it aids understanding; jargon is either avoided or explained; and the explanation is complete for the reader's standing question. Because there is no recommendation, the writer does not argue toward a verdict, and because the aim is to reveal rather than to move, the close is presentational and non-emoting. An explainer of how a suspension bridge carries load ends when the reader can picture the load path, not with a flourish urging them to feel anything about bridges.

## Technical documentation

**Shape: precision preserved, steps complete, easy to navigate.** The reader is trying to accomplish a task or understand a system exactly, often while consulting the document rather than reading it straight through.

Four demands stand out. Precision is inviolable: every technical term keeps its exact sense, and simplification may never silently narrow or widen a concept, because a reader will act on the words literally. Step completeness matters: a procedure must include every step and its steps must not overlap confusingly, since a gap becomes a failed task in the reader's hands. Picturability helps here too: the reader should be able to form a concrete image of what each step does. And navigability is a first-class concern: because the document is scanned and searched, its headings, ordering, and signposting must let a reader jump to what they need and trust that they have found it.

## Settings versus defaults: the human-ratifiable choices

Two of the choices above are not fixed by genre. They are *settings* the method exposes for a human to ratify, with sensible defaults but no hard-coded answer.

**Signposting density.** How much visible apparatus (explicit headings, numbering, indentation, inline transitions) a document should carry depends on genre, length, and how it is read, and reasonable practitioners land differently. A long reference document that is scanned and navigated warrants heavy, explicit scaffolding; a short piece read start to finish warrants light prose transitions only; an academic paper follows its venue's convention. The method's default is moderate (contentful headings at the real joints, minimal inline metadiscourse), but it treats density as a dial to be set for the case, not a constant. The one thing that never varies: any heading or topic sentence must carry a real claim, never a bare label.

**Whether the close pushes.** Whether an ending may reach for emotional or action-seeking force, or must stay flat and presentational, follows the document's purpose, and that too is a setting. The safe default is a presentational, non-emoting close (the writer reveals rather than moves) *unless* the brief explicitly establishes an action-seeking, decision-driving aim, which is the normal and legitimate case for business and analytical work. Academic, general-explanatory, and technical genres default to the presentational close.

Treating these two as settings rather than fixed defaults is deliberate. The genre narrows the range and supplies a default, but the final call (how much scaffolding, how hard the close pushes) is ratified by a human against the specific reader and purpose, not decided by the genre label alone. Everything else in this chapter is a genre parameter; these two are genre-informed dials left, on purpose, in human hands.
