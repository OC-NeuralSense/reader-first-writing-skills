# Glossary

Definitions of the functional terms used across this methodology. Each is
defined in the methodology's own terms and organized by writing function. For
how the terms fit together, see `./00-overview.md`.

---

## Core stance

**Reader-first premise**: The commitment that the final measure of a document
is whether the intended reader can understand it with the least effort, and that
this measure outranks correctness-on-the-page, elegance, and every internal
rule.

**Reader effort**: The cognitive work a reader must spend to recover the
writer's meaning: parsing sentences, holding context in working memory,
inferring unstated connections, and looking things up. The quantity the whole
methodology tries to minimize without altering meaning.

**Supreme criterion**: Reader comprehension (least reader effort). When two
meaning-preserving arrangements compete, the one that costs the intended reader
less effort wins, regardless of which plane proposed it.

---

## Reader and purpose

**Reader model**: An explicit, written estimate of the absent audience: who
they are, what they already know, the standing question they carry, and their
level of expertise. Both planes read from it; none may contradict it.

**Reader-frame**: The compact output of the orientation stage: the reader model
plus the communicative aim, the genre, and any flagged-uncertain assumptions.
The contract every downstream stage consumes.

**Standing question**: The question the reader already carries when they arrive.
The document exists to answer it, and its scope is the gap between what the
reader knows and what answering the question requires.

**Communicative aim**: What the writer intends to be true of the reader
afterward, principally whether the document means to *reveal a truth*
(presentational stance) or *seek an action* (may close with calibrated force).

**Mastered vocabulary**: The terms genuinely entrenched in the reader's
in-group, which may be used without definition. Vocabulary outside it must be
unpacked or replaced.

**Expert blind spot**: The way knowing a subject disables the writer's ability
to sense what a reader does not know; the gap conceals itself, so estimates of
shared knowledge run high. Countered by outside readers, cooling delay, and
tracing each term's first use, not by rereading.

---

## Structure plane

**Structure plane**: The document's logical architecture, everything above the
sentence: the governing question, the leading answer, the hierarchy of claims,
grouping, and ordering. Its product is a sound skeleton.

**Governing question**: The single question a document exists to answer, scaled
to the reader's knowledge gap. A document that turns on more than one reducible
question is really more than one document.

**Controlling idea**: The one-line answer to the governing question that leads
the document. It separates *subject* (what the document concerns) from *point*
(what is asserted about it); a controlling idea must carry a point, not just a
subject.

**Claim hierarchy**: The summary-before-detail arrangement in which each parent
claim is a genuine conclusion distilled from the claims beneath it, so a reader
can grasp the top and descend only as far as needed.

**Faithful summary**: A parent claim that genuinely says what its children add
up to. Its opposite is an *empty topper*: a heading that merely counts or names
its children ("three factors") without stating the insight they yield. Empty
toppers are disallowed.

**Grouping**: Collecting claims so that the members of a group are one logical
kind, do not overlap, and together cover the whole. A group nameable only by a
vague catch-all is usually mis-grouped.

**Formation-logic ordering**: Ordering a group's members by the analytical act
that formed it: a chain of reasoning, time sequence, part-structure, or degree
of importance, reconciled with the reader's processing load.

**Answer-first ordering**: Leading with the conclusion and placing its support
beneath it. The default, because it is lighter on the reader. Inverted to
*argument-first* only when the conclusion will meet the reader's resistance, so
the reasoning arrives before the unwelcome point.

---

## Prose plane

**Prose plane**: Sentence and paragraph cognition, everything at and below the
sentence: sentence shape and parsing, coherence, information flow, concreteness,
and word choice. Its product is readable surface that renders the skeleton
faithfully.

**Sentence geometry**: The structural shape of a sentence considered as a tree
that must be linearized into a word order the reader can parse without
backtracking. Covers branching direction, embedding depth, and misparse
("garden-path") traps.

**Coherence relation**: The logical connection a reader is meant to infer
between adjacent statements: cause, contrast, elaboration, example, and the
like. A relation is *recoverable* when the reader can reconstruct it without
guessing; an omitted or mislabeled connective breaks it.

**Given-before-new**: Arranging a sentence so that information the reader
already holds comes before information that is new, letting each new fact attach
to something already in mind. A high-priority default for integration.

**End-weight**: Placing the longest, most complex constituent last, so the
reader is not left holding a heavy phrase while waiting for the rest of the
sentence. Usually aligns with given-before-new; when they conflict, a reordering
construction is used to satisfy both.

**Imageability**: The degree to which wording lets the reader form a concrete
mental picture. "Can the reader picture this?" is used as a comprehension test,
and abstract summary is made concrete where a picture would aid grasp.

**Denominalization**: Turning an action that has been frozen into an abstract
noun back into a live verb, restoring a visible actor and action (e.g. "reach a
decision" → "decide").

**Topic string**: The thread of a recurring subject held steady across a
passage, typically in subject position, so the reader always knows whose story
the paragraph is telling.

## Diagnosis, revision, and fidelity

**Structure plane vs. prose plane diagnosis**: The practice of auditing
structural faults and prose faults on separate layers, because the two kinds of
failure are independent and route to different fixes.

**Diagnosis vs. correction**: The methodology separates *detecting* a defect
from *fixing* it: a diagnosis names a problem and the test it failed, but does
not automatically dictate one rewrite.

**Fidelity**: The invariant that no transformation, at any scale, may change the
propositional meaning. Reordering, voice change, summary, and plain-language
substitution must all hold roles, relations, and technical sense constant.
Fidelity overrides every other resolution.

**Technical precision (anchored)**: A preservation setting under which every
technical term keeps its exact concept through any substitution; simplification
may not silently narrow or widen it.

**Qualifier**: A hedge, scope limit, condition, or exception attached to a
claim. Qualifiers are retained by default; dropping one silently is a meaning
change, not a style improvement.

**Preservation controls**: The settings governing what a change may not move,
independent of how much effort the pass spends: *meaning* (strict/preserving),
*voice* (locked/envelope-bounded/open), *technical precision*
(anchored/standard), and *qualifiers* (retained/may-relax-low-stakes).

---

## Process and conventions

**Two-plane model**: The account of clarity as produced on two co-equal planes,
structure and prose, joined by the shared reader model, with reader
comprehension as the supreme arbiter between them.

**Writing lifecycle**: The nine functional stages (orient, architect, lay out,
render, diagnose, repair, reconcile, judge, release) that have a natural forward
order but loop: a defect returns control to the stage that owns it.

**Depth mode**: How thorough a pass is: *quick*, *standard*, *deep*,
*teaching*, or *audit*. Depth adds analysis and review machinery, never mere
length.

**Cross-plane arbitration**: The procedure for a same-passage conflict between a
faithful structural arrangement and a smoother prose one: try to satisfy both,
separate a real logical requirement from a preference, judge by reader effort,
and let fidelity override, escalating a genuinely unresolved case rather than
letting either plane win by default.

**Convention markers**: The strength labels used throughout: **[Principle]**
(load-bearing rule), **[Heuristic]** (context-dependent default), **[Diagnosis]**
(a way to detect, not to correct), and **[Invariant]** (a constraint no change
may violate).
