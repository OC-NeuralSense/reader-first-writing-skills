# Overview: A Reader-First Writing Methodology

This methodology is an independent synthesis of well-established ideas about how
documents get their logic and how sentences get their clarity. It is written
from scratch in its own terms; it is not a summary of any single source, and it
attributes no rule to any named author. What it offers is one coherent working
model for producing expository writing that a reader can understand with the
least effort.

Its intended range is practical nonfiction: business and analytical documents,
academic writing, general explanatory nonfiction, and technical documentation.
It does not address fiction, poetry, or oral and visual delivery.

---

## 1. The reader-first premise

Every decision in this methodology answers to one criterion: **can the intended
reader understand this with as little effort as possible?** Not "is it correct,"
not "is it elegant," not "is it complete on the page," but does it land in the
mind of the specific person you are writing for.

This reframes writing as an act performed on someone else's attention. The
writer already holds the whole idea; the reader receives it one word, one
sentence, one section at a time, through a narrow channel and a limited working
memory. Good writing is the discipline of respecting that channel. Wherever a
rule in this methodology seems to conflict with reader comprehension, reader
comprehension wins.

Two consequences follow, and they recur everywhere:

- **Comprehension is supreme, but meaning is inviolable.** We work relentlessly
  to reduce reader effort, yet never by altering what the document actually
  claims. Making something easier to read by making it say something subtly
  different is not a win; it is a defect (see *fidelity* in `./glossary.md`).
- **The writer is a poor judge of their own clarity.** Knowing the material
  makes it nearly impossible to feel what a newcomer does not know. This "expert
  blind spot" hides itself, so the methodology builds in outside checks rather
  than trusting the writer's sense that "this is obvious." See
  `./10-reader-and-purpose.md`.

## 2. The two-plane model

Clarity is produced on two distinct planes at once. This methodology treats them
as **co-equal, first-class sources of clarity**. Neither is a mere polish layer
on the other.

**The structure plane** is the document's logical architecture: everything
above the sentence. It owns the single question the document answers, the
one-line answer that leads it, the hierarchy of claims beneath that answer, how
claims are grouped, and the order within each group. Its product is a *sound
skeleton*: a set of claims arranged so that each supports the one above it and
the whole thing hangs together as an argument.

**The prose plane** is sentence and paragraph cognition: everything at and
below the sentence. It owns sentence shape and parsing, how ideas connect across
a boundary, the flow from familiar information to new, concreteness and
picturability, and word choice. Its product is *readable surface*: prose that
renders the skeleton without tripping the reader.

A document can fail on either plane independently. A flawless argument can be
buried in sentences no one can parse; graceful sentences can carry an argument
that does not actually hold together. Because the failures are independent, the
diagnoses and the fixes are kept on separate layers throughout this methodology.

**Why they are co-equal.** Structure is usually *worked out first*, because a
flaw in the skeleton is cheap to fix in an outline and expensive to fix once it
is dressed in finished prose. But "worked out first" is a scheduling fact, not a
ranking. Passing a structural check does not demote prose to a subordinate
touch-up. Both planes serve the same reader, and when they pull against each
other in the same passage, structure does **not** win by default (see §3).

**Why the prose plane is worth the effort.** Investing in the sentences, not only
in the skeleton, pays off three ways. First and foremost it *aids comprehension*:
well-made prose lowers the reader's effort directly, which is the whole criterion
above. Second, it *signals care*: prose that has plainly been worked on tells the
reader the writer took both the subject and the reader seriously, and that
impression of diligence earns credibility the argument then trades on. Third, it
can give *aesthetic pleasure*: a well-turned sentence is simply satisfying to
read, and a reader who is enjoying the passage stays with it longer and more
willingly.

One question inside this stays deliberately open. Whether elegance is *itself a
source of clarity* (whether a more beautiful sentence is, for that reason, an
easier one to understand) or whether elegance only buys pleasure and trust while
clarity comes from structure and cognition alone, is not settled here. This
methodology adopts the integrated view as its working stance: it treats the two
planes as co-equal sources of clarity rather than ranking prose beneath structure.
But that is a ratifiable position, open to a human to affirm or revise, not a
proven fact, flagged here so the choice is visible rather than smuggled in.

**The shared reader model joins them.** Both planes read from a single estimate
of the audience: what they already know, the question they are carrying, their
level of expertise, and the writer's aim toward them. That one estimate sets the
depth of the structure and the calibration of the prose: how much can be
assumed, which terms can go undefined, how densely to signpost. No plane may
contradict the shared reader model. Building it is the subject of
`./10-reader-and-purpose.md`.

## 3. When the two planes collide

Occasionally a structurally faithful arrangement reads worse than an arrangement
that would disturb the logic, in the very same passage, and no rewrite satisfies
both. This methodology does **not** resolve that by decree. The procedure is:

1. **Try to satisfy both.** Most apparent collisions dissolve with a
   near-equivalent construction: change the voice, front a phrase, reorder
   constituents, split one sentence into two, or repeat a keyword across the
   boundary. Reach here first.
2. **Separate a real logical requirement from a mere preference.** A parent that
   must genuinely summarize its children, groups that must not overlap: these
   are load-bearing. Signpost density or a choice between two already-valid
   orders is not.
3. **Judge by reader effort.** Between two arrangements that both preserve
   meaning, keep the one the intended reader grasps with less work, whichever
   plane proposed it.
4. **Fidelity overrides everything.** If the only way to reconcile them changes
   the meaning, neither plane wins; the passage is escalated for rethinking, not
   quietly reworded.

There is deliberately no standing "structure beats prose" or "prose beats
structure" rule. A genuine, unresolved same-passage conflict is flagged for a
human decision, not settled by the system.

## 4. The writing lifecycle (non-linear)

Writing does not run once through a fixed pipeline. The methodology names nine
functional stages that have a natural forward order but loop freely: a defect
found late sends control back to whichever stage owns it.

1. **Orient**: fix the situation, the reader, and the purpose. *(shared plane)*
2. **Architect**: reduce the document to one governing question, state the
   one-line answer, inventory and screen the claims, and build them into a
   grouped, ordered hierarchy. *(structure plane)*
3. **Lay out**: map the validated hierarchy onto sections and paragraphs,
   design the opening, and plan the bridges between blocks. *(bridge / shared)*
4. **Render**: draft continuous prose from the layout, introducing no claim the
   structure does not already contain; treat the draft as raw material.
   *(prose plane)*
5. **Diagnose**: audit for defects, keeping structural faults and prose faults
   on separate layers, each finding tied to a concrete failed test.
6. **Repair**: apply the fixes, structural and prose, holding meaning constant.
7. **Reconcile**: confirm every transformation preserved meaning, and resolve
   any cross-plane collision by the ladder in §3. *(shared plane)*
8. **Judge**: assess the whole against reader-facing goals: comprehension,
   soundness, credibility, correctness. *(shared plane)*
9. **Release**: settle disputed usage and punctuation, set apparatus to the
   genre, and ship; a late defect re-enters at its owning stage.

Characteristic loops: a structural fault found in **Diagnose** reopens
**Architect**; a stubborn local incoherence that turns out to be a missing link
is escalated from the prose layer to the structure layer rather than patched; a
review that reveals the audience was misjudged reopens **Orient** and re-flows to
both planes; a meaning change caught in **Reconcile** rolls back the repair.

## 5. Depth modes and preservation controls (conceptual)

The same stage can be run at different intensities. **Depth** governs how
thorough a pass is:

- **quick**: one fast surface pass; explicitly non-exhaustive and never a
  quality gate.
- **standard**: a normal pass with its own diagnose-and-repair loop.
- **deep**: adds independent, blind review and extra analysis passes; more
  machinery, not more words.
- **teaching**: every move is shown with its defect, the test it failed, and
  the reason behind the fix, so a learner can re-apply the test alone.
- **audit**: a release-oriented go/no-go that separates blocking from
  non-blocking defects.

**Preservation controls** govern how tightly a change must hold the original
constant, independent of depth:

- **meaning**: from *strict* (zero propositional drift permitted) to
  *preserving* (equivalent claims, freer wording).
- **voice**: from *locked* through *envelope-bounded* to *open*.
- **technical precision**: *anchored* (every technical term keeps its exact
  concept through any substitution) or *standard*.
- **qualifiers**: hedges, scope limits, and conditions are *retained* by
  default, and may be relaxed only where stakes are low and stated to be so.

Depth is about effort spent; preservation is about what may not move. A `quick`
pass and a `deep` pass can both be `strict` on meaning.

## 6. Strong principles vs. optional heuristics

Not every guideline carries the same force. This methodology marks the
difference explicitly and uses the convention throughout:

- **[Principle]**: a load-bearing rule that holds across the v1 range. Breaking
  it usually produces a real comprehension or soundness failure. Example: *state
  the answer before its support unless the reader will resist it.*
- **[Heuristic]**: a default that is right more often than not, but is
  conditioned on genre, audience, or context and yields to judgment. Example:
  *prefer the shorter of two equally clear phrasings.*
- **[Diagnosis]**: a way to *detect* a problem. A diagnosis names a defect; it
  does not automatically dictate the fix. We keep detection separate from
  correction so that finding "this is hard to parse" does not force one
  particular rewrite.
- **[Invariant]**: a constraint no transformation may violate at any stage,
  chiefly meaning preservation and argument soundness.

Two distinctions ride alongside these markers, and the methodology keeps them
apart on purpose:

- **A logical weakness is not a stylistic preference.** "This claim does not
  follow" is a soundness defect; "this sentence is graceless" is a matter of
  craft. They route to different fixes.
- **Complexity is not needless difficulty.** Some ideas are inherently hard;
  the goal is to remove *avoidable* effort, not to flatten genuine complexity
  into something false.

When a statement carries no marker, read it as explanatory context, not as a
rule to enforce.

## 7. How to read this methodology

The files are layered by progressive disclosure. Read only as deep as your task
needs.

- Start here (`00-overview.md`) for the premise, the two planes, and the
  lifecycle.
- `./10-reader-and-purpose.md`: build the reader model and define purpose; this
  precedes and parameterizes everything downstream.
- The structure-plane files (e.g. `./20-argument-architecture.md`) cover the
  governing question, the hierarchy, grouping, and ordering.
- The prose-plane files cover sentence geometry, coherence, information flow,
  concreteness, and word choice.
- The diagnosis, revision, and review files cover finding faults, fixing them
  under the fidelity invariant, and judging the result for release.
- `./glossary.md`: precise definitions of every functional term used above.

Sibling files are produced independently; cross-references use relative paths.
When two files seem to give conflicting advice, the tie-breakers are, in order:
the fidelity invariant, then reader comprehension, then the marked strength of
the guideline (`[Principle]` over `[Heuristic]`).

The binding statement of authority for the whole system, including the full
order of authority, what may never override this methodology, and the
prohibited shortcuts, is the writing constitution in
`./constitution/writing-constitution.md`. Every skill, agent, and workflow
operates under it; it is not negotiable at runtime.
